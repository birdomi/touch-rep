## Findings

**1. ID: pretraining now wins on both tasks, and slip is no longer ambiguous.** With one seed the
slip gain sat inside the fold noise; across three seeds it is +0.0222 macro F1 with a seed spread
of 0.005–0.006 and 3/3 seed-level wins. Note the caveat below on what this spread measures.

**2. OOD averages are a wash on grasp and mildly favour `local_e80` on slip** — but the average is
the wrong summary here, because the per-object variance dwarfs the encoder difference
(scratch grasp: ±0.16 across objects vs a −0.008 encoder gap).

**3. The real OOD effect is worst-case rescue, not average lift.** On both tasks `local_e80`
loses modestly on the objects scratch already handles and wins decisively on the object scratch
fails. Grasp `driver`: scratch 0.4890 — essentially chance — while `local_e80` reaches 0.6318
(+0.1429). Slip `doll`: scratch 0.5347 → `local_e80` 0.6331 (+0.0984). Scratch wins 3/5 grasp
objects and 3/4 slip objects, yet its floor is far lower. Pretraining is buying robustness on the
hard tail, not uniform accuracy.

**4. `local_e80` is also more consistent across objects on grasp** (±0.0998 vs ±0.1625 macro F1),
consistent with the same interpretation.

**5. OOD is much harder than ID, as expected.** Grasp drops 0.8617 → 0.7424 and slip 0.6685 →
0.5960 for `local_e80`. Several slip OOD cells (0.54–0.63) are close to the 0.5 floor.

## Caveats

- **The `±` in the ID table is seed spread with the episode split held fixed** (`split_seed=42`),
  so it measures training-run stability, not data variability. Fold-to-fold spread — the larger
  source of uncertainty — is in the CSV (`last_balacc_std`, `last_f1macro_std`) and runs
  0.01–0.04. Conclusions stated as "outside the noise" hold against seed noise; a full claim needs
  varied split seeds too.
- Each OOD cell is a **single train/val split** (one held-out object), not a K-fold mean, so its
  per-seed variance is higher than the ID numbers.
- One backbone LR (1e-4) only. The earlier LR sweep showed the encoder ranking on slip can flip at
  1e-6, so these conclusions are specific to 1e-4.
- `base_e100` was not run here; see `results_lr_matrix_20260727_180932.md` for that comparison.
